Design notes

Why it looks like this

The key comes first

Every row shows the short key; every contact page shows the whole key in groups of four, big enough to read aloud, with Copy and their Isol QR. Names can change or be faked; the key can't.

Key colours

Eight swatches read straight from the key. Two keys that differ look different at a glance, which helps spot a look-alike before you compare the full key.

Two names, clearly labelled

The big name is yours to edit ("only you see this"). Under it: what they call themselves. If you haven't named someone, their own name shows in quotes, so it never looks like something you confirmed.

Verified ≠ trusted

Verified (Gilt) is about the key: you scanned their QR or compared keys in person, and the book remembers how and when. Trusted (Olive) is about the person. Either, both, or neither; Melot is verified but not trusted.

Same name, different key

When an unverified contact calls themselves the same as someone you've verified, the row gets a red mark and the page says so, with a way to compare the two keys.

Adding someone

Scan their QR, paste a key, or pick someone from a chat. The key is checked as you type, its colours appear, and if it's already in your book you're told who it is.
